What is a water-sensitive city?

A city that manages rain, rivers, groundwater and wastewater as one cycle, storing rain where it falls instead of draining it away.

Three cities, three water failures

Bengaluru

When development breaks the lake system
Karnataka, India
6,900/ 14,000
borewells dry · March 2024
Sept 2022: Outer Ring Road flooded

Technical

  • Linked lakes (keres) disconnected
  • Water bodies lost since the 1970s
  • Aquifers over-pumped across the city

Political and social

  • Wetlands sold after the IT boom
  • Evictions target settlers, not campuses
  • The periphery depends on tankers

Solutions

  • Reconnect the lake cascade
  • One authority for lakes, drains and sewage
  • Secure tenure first; enforce rules on the powerful

Delhi

One month, two water crises
NCT, India
228 mm
of rain in 24 hours · 28 June 2024
July: basement library deaths, Old Rajinder Nagar

Technical

  • Most raw water comes from other states
  • High non-revenue water losses in the network
  • Drains are covered and clogged

Political and social

  • Supply is contested across state lines
  • Water moves by political pressure
  • Vendors cleared; basements carry more risk

Solutions

  • Metered districts; recharge the floodplain
  • Adopt the water policy stalled since 2011
  • Equal supply across zones and colonies

Zhengzhou

When sponge design meets extreme rain
Henan, China
201.9 mm
of rain in one hour · 20 July 2021
380 dead or missing

Technical

  • Sponge measures weaken as storms intensify
  • Days of rain approach a year's total
  • No safe overflow route for metro or tunnels

Political and social

  • Warnings ignored; deaths concealed
  • Sponge budgets went to landscaping
  • The city was sold as a climate hero

Solutions

  • Pair green with grey; floodproof the metro
  • A red alert should stop the metro
  • Publish casualty and risk data

What a just water-sensitive city must do

  1. Plan by watershed, not by plot
  2. Combine green and grey infrastructure
  3. Count informal systems as infrastructure
  4. Enforce rules equally; secure tenure first
  5. Publish the data
