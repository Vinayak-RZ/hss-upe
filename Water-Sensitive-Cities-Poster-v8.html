What is a water-sensitive city?

A city that runs rain, rivers, groundwater and wastewater as one cycle, and plans its land around where water goes. It holds rain where it falls, recharges the ground and reuses what it drains. Cities tend to move from simply supplying water, to sewering and draining it, to finally working with the whole cycle.

Bengaluru’s lake cascade once did exactly this. Pipes and drains pushed it backwards: now it both floods and runs dry.

Political and social explanation

  • Lakes became land: once piped water arrived, lakes looked like empty land to build on.
  • Selective enforcement: “encroachment” rules evict settlers on filled channels, but not a multinational on wetland next door.
  • Unequal supply: only 10–30% of periphery homes had piped water; tanker “mafias” work alongside officials.
  • Rules as a choice: the state picks when its rules apply. The 2024 tanker price cap stayed on paper.

Solutions for Bengaluru

One answer in each register: the pipes, the offices, and who the water reaches.

Technocratic

  • Reconnect lake cascades and protect the valleys they drain.
  • Refill lakes with treated wastewater, a large untapped source.
  • Build recharge wells so rain soaks back into the ground.

Bureaucratic

  • One catchment authority for lakes, storm drains and sewage.
  • Register and meter every tanker, and publish the data.
  • Enforce the tanker price cap, not only announce it.

Political ecology

  • Apply encroachment rules to corporate and state projects first.
  • Secure tenure before any eviction for lake or drain works.
  • Give lake users and the periphery a seat in restoration.

Conclusion

Bengaluru once held its rain. Politics, not just hydrology, broke the cascade. A water-sensitive Bengaluru must rebuild the lakes and the rules together.

The right to the city includes the power to decide where water goes and whom it reaches.
